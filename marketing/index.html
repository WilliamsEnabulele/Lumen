How it goes

Three things happen, in this order.

01 — Upload

You hand over a document

A chapter, a set of slides, lecture notes. PDF, Word, PowerPoint or plain text. If it turns out to be a scan with no text in it, Lumen says so and tells you to run it through OCR rather than quietly building a course out of nothing.

02 — It reads

The model decides the lessons

Not a table of contents scraped off the headings. It reads the material and works out what has to be understood before what, then grounds every concept in the passage it came from — so anything it says can be traced back.

03 — It teaches

Out loud, and it waits for you

Every turn is generated in the moment, not read off a script. It draws while it explains, asks a question when it thinks the idea has landed, then stops talking and gives you twelve seconds of silence to think in.

The board

It illustrates while it talks.

The tutor has eight things it can put on the board, and it chooses them mid-sentence — the way a lecturer turns around and writes something down. Nothing is pre-rendered from the document.

Every one is checked before it is drawn. Ask to highlight line nine of a four-line program and the request comes back as an error, because a board that quietly does the wrong thing is worse than one that refuses.

show_statementThe line worth leaving up — a definition, a rule, the claim being made.

Interruptions

You can talk over it.

Cut in mid-sentence and it stops — actually stops, at the word you cut in on. It deals with what you said, then picks the thread back up from the start of the clause rather than the middle of a word, because re-entering mid-word sounds like a stutter instead of a teacher carrying on.

What it knows about you

It keeps the receipts.

Lumen holds a number for every concept: how confident it is that you have it. That number decides whether you get taught something a second time, so you can open it and see exactly which answers moved it and by how much.

Saying nothing never counts against you. A student who went quiet has told it nothing about what they know, and marking that as failure would punish thinking.

And when it gives up on a concept, it says so instead of burying it.

How it talks

It meets you where you speak.

Lumen opens in standard English at everybody. If you drop into Pidgin, it notices — and only after you have done it twice does it start throwing one back. Never in a definition, never in a question it is marking you on. The terms stay precise, because you will be examined in them.

Tell it to speak plainly and it does, immediately, for good. Nothing you say afterwards re-opens it.

You

Abeg, explain am again — I no sabi this one at all.

Tutor

No wahala. Look at the board again — forget the code for a second. A loop is a promise about when it stops. That's the whole hard part.

You

Ehen. So the condition is the promise.

Tutor

Exactly that. And a condition nothing inside the body can ever make false is a loop that runs forever.

Questions

Before you upload anything.

What can I actually give it?

PDF, Word, PowerPoint, plain text and Markdown, up to 32MB. If a PDF turns out to be a scan or a photograph of pages, it tells you it needs OCR first instead of building an empty course and letting you find out later.

Do I need a microphone?

No. It reads aloud either way, and you can type anything you'd have said. With a microphone you also get to cut in mid-sentence, which is the part people end up using most.

Is it just reading my document back to me?

No. Every turn is generated live against what you just said and what is already on the board — so asking a question changes what comes next. What it can't do is go outside your material: ask it something the document doesn't cover and it says so rather than answering from general knowledge.

What if I get something wrong?

It explains it again from a different angle — a different example, a different order, a picture instead of words. After two goes it moves on and records that the concept didn't land, rather than holding you in a loop you can't leave.

Who else sees my documents?

Nobody. They are yours, they are used to build your course, and your progress is yours to look at. Uploads are stored under opaque keys that carry no file name.
